The Mad Hatter Privacy Policy

Last updated: 4 November 2026

The Mad Hatter saves your game progress on your device. You do not need a SmoothSteve account to play. Some versions include Google Play Games. In version 1.45, eligible players can optionally connect a Google Play Games profile for leaderboards. The game also uses Google's advertising and consent services as described below. SmoothSteve does not operate a game server or cloud-save service for The Mad Hatter.

Age information

Age setup varies by version. Version 1.30 asks you to choose an age group: under 13, 13-17, or 18 or older. It does not ask for a date of birth. Versions 1.38 and 1.45 ask for a date of birth to work out an age group on the device. In those versions, the game discards the entered date after the check and does not save or send that date to SmoothSteve or Google. It saves only a broad category: child (13 or younger), teen (14-17), or adult (18 or older).

The category controls advertising treatment and, where supported, access to Play Games. Age-related advertising and consent signals can be sent to Google; these are separate from the discarded birth-date entry.

Information saved on your device

Depending on the version, local data includes personal-best scores, achievement progress, unlocked and equipped hats, Garden growth and unlocks, daily goals and earned stamps, returning-player progress, settings, the broad age category, and leaderboard participation and notice choices. The game also keeps local integrity and recovery files and may create a score-sharing image when you request one.

Connecting to leaderboards does not back up or synchronize hats, Garden or other local progress. In versions 1.38 and 1.45, Android cloud backup and device-transfer backup are disabled for the game's own app data. Copies you deliberately share or store elsewhere are separate.

Google Play Games and optional leaderboards

Version 1.45 keeps Play Games off until an eligible player chooses to connect. The following leaderboard description applies to that version. Google manages the Google account and Play Games profile used for this feature; it may present its own sign-in or profile-setup screens. No separate SmoothSteve game account is created. The game remembers your participation choice on this device across playthroughs, restarts and updates.

After connecting, newly completed eligible runs can send the mode, score and a submission identifier to Google Play Games, linked to your gamer identity. Runs must begin after eligible participation and finish online. This version does not upload saved personal bests or replay runs completed before connecting or while offline. Manual-only participation, where previously chosen, remains manual-only. A request already sent to Google may finish after an interruption.

Google compares submitted scores within daily, weekly and all-time periods. Historical scores already stored by Google may remain. When you open rankings, the game fetches public gamer display names, ranks and scores from Google. The game's embedded list shows only the public all-time top five for the selected mode. A missing row does not reveal whether a player's profile is private or why their score is absent.

Public rankings include profiles that share gameplay publicly. Changing profile visibility is optional and is controlled by Google, not by The Mad Hatter. Google Play Games handles gamer identity and service data and may process SDK usage, analytics and diagnostics. See Google's Play Games data-disclosure guidance and Google's Privacy Policy.

In version 1.45, turning Play Games participation off blocks new requests from the game's leaderboard feature and forgets the local participation choice. It does not sign you out of Google, stop requests already sent, guarantee that Google's initialized SDK immediately stops all processing, or delete Google-held scores/profile data.

Older version 1.38 can initialize Play Games and check authentication for eligible teen/adult sessions without the newer participation choice. It also offers a Connect action in Settings when the service is available; Google may present its own profile-setup screen for an explicit connection. Its released configuration does not have working leaderboard identifiers. The version 1.45 opt-in controls and run-only submission rules should not be assumed to exist in every older version. Google's SDK can process service usage and diagnostic data when initialized, even where a leaderboard is unavailable.

Advertising and consent

The Mad Hatter uses Google Mobile Ads (AdMob) for a banner and Google's User Messaging Platform to manage consent and privacy choices. Versions 1.38 and 1.45 do not request ads for their child category. For eligible older players, the game sets Google's age-treatment options and asks Google's consent service whether ads may be requested. When Google requires a consent message, it is displayed before the game requests an ad. Requested ad content is limited to General Audience content. Older version 1.30 can also request a banner for child sessions if the consent SDK permits it; it applies the corresponding child or teen advertising treatment and marks child and teen consent requests as under the age of consent. Version 1.30 does not have the newer child-category ad-request block.

The game does not request Android location permissions. Google's advertising services can still estimate approximate location from the device's IP address. Depending on age and privacy settings, Google and advertising buyers or partners can process approximate location, device/account identifiers, app or ad interactions, and diagnostics for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention. AdMob may use advertising buyers even when the game has no separately integrated third-party ad-network SDK. Consent handling can send device/app information, saved consent information and age-treatment signals to Google. We do not send the entered birth date to these services. Google describes its own handling in its Mobile Ads data disclosure, consent-service information and Privacy Policy.

The game's age and consent checks control its ad requests. They are not a promise that every Google SDK component performs no processing before those checks, or that declining personalized ads stops all service data handling.

The game provides Privacy & ads when Google requires an ongoing privacy-options entry point; its location can vary by version, including the title screen or Settings. Where available, that control lets you revisit Google's privacy choices. Android also provides advertising-ID controls. Declining personalized advertising is different from disabling every advertising-service data request.

Sharing a score

If you choose Share score, the game creates score text or an image and opens Android's chooser. The shared content can include the run's score, mode, personal best and selected hat. You choose the destination app and complete sharing there. The Mad Hatter does not automatically post the card or send it to a SmoothSteve game server. Copies sent to another service are handled under that service's rules.

Retention and deletion

Local progress stays in the game's app storage until you clear its data or uninstall. Clearing that storage permanently removes local progress and choices; it does not delete Google-held scores or copies shared elsewhere. No SmoothSteve cloud backup can restore it.

To manage or delete Google Play Games data for The Mad Hatter, use Google's Delete individual game data controls. Google's instructions are available at Delete your Play Games data or profile, including the web route at Play Games profile. You do not need to delete your whole Google account to use the individual-game controls. Deleting Play Games data is separate from Google's Play History and advertising data, which have their own controls and retention policies.

Turning leaderboards off is not a deletion request. SmoothSteve has no separate game-server profile to remove. Contact us if you need help understanding which data is local or managed by Google. Google's services retain their data under Google's policies; we do not promise a fixed deletion or retention period on Google's behalf.

Security

Google states that the advertising data described in its Mobile Ads disclosure is encrypted in transit and that Play Games service data uses HTTPS. The bundled consent SDK's main consent request also uses HTTPS. These statements do not mean local save files are encrypted or that The Mad Hatter has received an independent security certification.
