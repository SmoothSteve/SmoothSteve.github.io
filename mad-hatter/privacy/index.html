The Mad Hatter Privacy Policy

Last updated: 5 November 2026

The Mad Hatter saves your game progress on your device. You do not need a SmoothSteve account to play. Eligible players can choose to connect Google Play Games for leaderboards. This policy describes the current connection flow, including the reconnect correction introduced in version 1.47, and explains important differences in older releases. The game also uses Google's advertising and consent services. SmoothSteve does not operate a game server, separate player-account system or cloud-save service for The Mad Hatter.

Age information

In the current date-of-birth setup, the game calculates an age category on your device and discards the entered date after the check. It does not save or send that date to SmoothSteve or Google. It saves only a broad category: child (13 or younger), teen (14–17) or adult (18 or older). Version 1.38 uses this same approach. Historical version 1.30 instead asks you to select under 13, 13–17 or 18 and older; it does not request a date of birth.

The category controls advertising treatment and, where supported, access to Play Games. The game's child or unknown-age category cannot connect to Play Games. Derived age-treatment and consent signals can be sent to Google; these are separate from the discarded birth-date entry.

Information saved on your device

Local data can include personal bests, achievement progress, unlocked and equipped hats, Garden growth and unlocks, daily goals and earned stamps, returning-player progress, settings, the broad age category, and leaderboard participation and notice choices. The game also keeps local integrity and recovery files and can create a score-sharing image when you request one.

Connecting to leaderboards does not back up or synchronize hats, Garden or other local progress. The current Android build disables cloud backup and device-transfer backup of its own app data, as does version 1.38. Local recovery files are not a cloud backup. Copies you deliberately share or store elsewhere are separate.

Google Play Games and optional leaderboards

With the current connection flow, Play Games stays off until an eligible player chooses to connect and acknowledges the current connection information. Google manages the Google account and Play Games profile used for this feature and may show its own sign-in or profile-setup screens. No separate SmoothSteve game account is created. The game remembers your participation choice on this device across playthroughs, restarts and updates when it can save that choice successfully.

Updated connection information can require renewed review. The updated flow retains an older participation choice but waits for you to review and confirm before reconnecting. Cancel or Back leaves local play available and does not broaden an earlier manual-only sharing choice. Once the current information has been acknowledged, later launches can reconnect quietly. If saving a choice fails, the game shows a warning; remembering it cannot then be guaranteed.

After connecting, newly completed eligible runs can send their mode, score and submission identifier to Google Play Games, linked to your gamer identity. A run must begin after eligible connection and finish online. Automatic participants share each eligible completed run, even if it is below or equal to their local personal best. An older manual-only choice remains manual-only and requires the results-screen submission action. Saved personal bests, pre-connection runs and offline runs are not uploaded later.

The game allows a short wait for a newly completed online run's request. This is not an offline upload queue or a cloud backup. A request already handed to Google may finish after an interruption or period boundary. Only a matching acknowledgement confirms that Google accepted the request; a timeout or unavailable connection is not shown as success.

Google compares scores within daily, weekly and all-time periods. Scores already held by Google may remain. When you open rankings, the game fetches public gamer display names, ranks and scores. The embedded list shows only the public all-time top five for the selected mode or course. Google's separate leaderboard view provides its available period controls. A missing row does not identify a private profile or diagnose why a score is absent.

To appear publicly, a profile must share gameplay publicly through Google's visibility controls. Changing visibility is optional. You can connect while keeping your profile private; The Mad Hatter does not detect or change that setting. Google's service handles gamer identity and service data and can process SDK usage, analytics and diagnostics. See Google's Play Games disclosure and Google's Privacy Policy.

Turning participation off blocks new requests from the game's leaderboard feature and saves participation as Off on this device. It does not sign you out of Google, stop requests already sent, guarantee that Google's initialized SDK immediately stops every operation, or delete Google-held scores or profile data.

Older versions behave differently. Version 1.38 can initialize Play Games and check authentication for eligible teen/adult sessions without the newer participation choice. Its Settings Connect route can show Google profile setup, but its released configuration has no working leaderboard identifiers. Google's initialized SDK can process usage and diagnostics even when a leaderboard is unavailable.

Version 1.46 added the renewed connection review, but an older remembered choice could still start the native SDK before that review. Version 1.47 corrected this startup gate. The current opt-in, renewed-review and run-only rules do not apply to every older release.

Advertising and consent

The Mad Hatter uses Google Mobile Ads (AdMob) for a banner and Google's User Messaging Platform for consent and privacy choices. The current build does not request ads for its child category; version 1.38 also blocks those requests. For eligible older players, the game sets Google's age-treatment options and asks the consent service whether ads may be requested. If Google requires a consent message, it appears before the game requests an ad. Requested ad content is limited to General Audience content.

From version 1.46, teen ad requests use Google's CHILD-level restricted treatment, together with an under-age-of-consent signal to the consent service. CHILD is Google's advertising-treatment label; it does not change a teen's saved age category or Play Games eligibility. Version 1.38 uses Google's different TEEN treatment. Google's consent-service age flag and its advertising-treatment setting are separate.

Historical version 1.30 can request a banner for its child sessions if the consent SDK permits it, with its corresponding age-treatment flags; it lacks the newer child-category ad-request block. Alpha distribution of 1.30 was paused on 5 November 2026. That does not erase older installations.

The game does not request Android location permissions. Advertising services can still estimate approximate location from an IP address. Depending on age and privacy settings, Google and advertising buyers or partners can process approximate location, device/account identifiers, app or ad interactions, and diagnostics for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention. AdMob can use advertising buyers without a separately integrated third-party ad-network SDK. Consent handling can send device/app information, saved consent information and age-treatment signals to Google. We do not send the entered birth date. See Google's Mobile Ads disclosure, consent-service information and Privacy Policy.

The game's checks control its ad requests. They are not a promise that every Google SDK component performs no processing before those checks, or that declining personalized ads stops all service data handling.

The game provides Privacy & ads when Google requires an ongoing privacy-options entry point. Its location varies by version, including the title screen or Settings. Where available, use it to revisit Google's privacy choices. Android also provides advertising-ID controls. Declining personalized advertising is different from disabling every advertising-service request. From version 1.46, the full policy is also available offline in Settings and before age entry; reading it does not connect to Play Games.

Sharing a score

If you choose Share score, the game creates score text or an image and opens Android's chooser. Content can include the run's score, mode, personal best and selected hat. You choose the destination and complete sharing there. The Mad Hatter does not automatically post it or upload it to a SmoothSteve game server. Copies sent elsewhere are handled under the destination service's rules.

Retention and deletion

Local progress stays in app storage until you clear its data or uninstall. Doing so permanently removes local progress and choices; it does not delete Google-held scores or shared copies. No SmoothSteve cloud backup can restore it.

To manage or delete Google Play Games data for The Mad Hatter, use Google's Delete individual game data controls. See Delete your Play Games data or profile, including the web route at Play Games profile. You do not need to delete your whole Google account to use individual-game controls. Play Games data is separate from Google's Play History and advertising data, which have their own controls and retention rules.

Turning leaderboards off is not a deletion request. SmoothSteve has no separate game-server account to remove. Contact us for help understanding which data is local or managed by Google. Google's services retain their data under Google's policies; we do not promise a fixed retention or deletion deadline on Google's behalf.

Security

Google states that advertising data covered by its Mobile Ads disclosure is encrypted in transit and Play Games service data uses HTTPS. The bundled consent SDK's main request also uses HTTPS. This does not mean local saves are encrypted or the game has received independent security certification.
